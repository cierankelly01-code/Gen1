Guide

Hemmed edges explained.

A hemmed edge folds the sheet back on itself. It removes the sharp edge, stiffens the panel and cuts out a finishing operation.

In short: A hemmed, or safety, edge is the edge of a sheet folded back on itself to close it. It removes the sharp edge for operators and end users, doubles the material at the edge so the panel is stiffer, and replaces deburring or linishing. Genex hems mild steel, stainless and aluminium in a single hit.

01

Three jobs at once

  • Safety: no sharp edge for your operators, your assembly line or the end user.
  • Strength: doubling the material at the edge stiffens the panel, so you can often keep a thinner gauge instead of stepping up a thickness.
  • Finish: a tidy, closed edge that looks like a finished product, not a cut blank.

And it removes a whole secondary operation: no linishing, no deburring cell, no edge trim sent out and back.

02

Where hemmed edges are used

Panels and covers people handle, guards, enclosures, trays and anything where a cut edge would need deburring or a separate edge trim.

03

Designing for a hem

Allow extra flat length for the material that folds back, keep holes and features clear of the hem, and tell us whether the edge needs to be fully closed or can have a small gap. Send the drawing and we'll confirm what's achievable in your material and thickness.

The edge of a sheet folded back on itself to close it, also called a safety edge.

Which materials can be hemmed?

We hem mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium.

Is hemming only for production runs?

No. We hem everything from one-off prototypes to repeat production batches.
