02 · Process

How a progression tool makes your part

Steel coil feeds through the tool one pitch per press stroke. Each station does one job: pilot holes locate the strip, then the tool pierces, notches, forms and inserts, and the last station cuts the finished part free. A part drops out on every stroke, which is why pressing gives the lowest price per part at volume.

Simpler parts, or lower volumes, run in single-action tools (one operation per stroke) or multi-stage tools, with the operator moving the part between stages. That keeps tooling cost down when a progression tool wouldn't pay back.

04 · Route

When pressing makes sense

Pressing pays off once volumes reach several thousand parts a year and the design is settled, because the production tool is a one-off cost spread across every part. Below that, or while the design is still changing, laser cutting and folding is usually cheaper. Near the break point we price both, so you can compare. See pressing vs laser cut and fold.

05 · Design

Design tips for pressed parts

  • Keep holes at least one material thickness across, and at least 1.5× thickness from an edge
  • Keep holes away from bends (at least 2× thickness plus the bend radius), or they'll distort
  • Use generous inside bend radii, especially in stainless, aluminium and high-strength steel
  • Mark the burr side on the drawing if it matters for fit or handling
  • Specify tight tolerances only where the function needs them: they drive tool cost
  • Tell us if nuts or studs are needed: they can often go in during pressing

Starting guidelines, not limits. Tell us what a feature has to do and we'll tell you what's achievable. More in our design guide.

Questions

Frequently asked questions

How thick can you press?

Up to 5 mm, on mechanical presses up to 200 tonnes, depending on the material and the size of the part.

What volumes do you press?

From a few hundred parts to millions a year. Coil-fed progression tools are used for the long runs.

Can nuts and studs go in during pressing?

Yes. Nuts and studs can be inserted in the press tool, or as a separate operation in our PEM and projection welding cells.

Can I get prototypes before committing to tooling?

Yes. Prototypes are usually laser cut and folded with no tooling cost, so you can prove the design before the production tool is made.

Which materials can you press?

Mild steel, stainless steel, aluminium and coated steels such as pre-galvanised. Tell us the grade and we'll confirm.

Who maintains the press tools?

We do, in our own toolroom in Walsall, with preventative maintenance and fast response if a tool needs attention.

Can you take over parts from another supplier?

Yes. Send the drawing, volumes and details of the existing tooling, and we'll assess the best way to bring the part to Genex.
