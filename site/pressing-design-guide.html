Designing pressed steel parts that tool well and run well

Quick answer

Use laser cutting and folding below about 2,000 parts a year, stage or compound tooling from roughly 2,000 to 20,000, and progressive tooling from around 10,000 parts a year upwards on multi-year programmes. Design holes at least one material thickness in diameter and keep them at least 2× thickness from edges and bends.

Choosing a manufacturing route by volume

Typical routes for a steel bracket, 0.5–3.0 mm
RouteTypical annual volumeToolingPiece price
Laser cut & press brake fold1 – 2,000NoneHighest
Single-stage / compound tools2,000 – 20,000ModerateMedium
Progressive tool10,000 – 250,000+HighestLowest

The bands overlap on purpose. The right answer depends on part size, the number of forming operations and programme life. A common, low-risk path is laser-cut prototypes for design validation, then production tooling once the design is frozen.

FeatureGuideline
Minimum pierced hole diameter≥ 1.0 t (≥ 1.5 t for high-strength steels)

Hole to bend≥ 2 t + inside bend radius
Hole to hole≥ 2 t
Inside bend radius≥ 1 t mild steel · 1.5–2 t HSLA · check stainless & aluminium temper
Minimum flange length≥ 4 t
Bend reliefWidth ≥ t, depth ≥ bend radius + t
Slot width≥ 1.5 t
Burr directionSpecify the burr side on the drawing if it matters for fit or handling

These are starting points, not limits. With the right tooling we regularly go tighter. Tell us the function of the feature and we'll tell you what's achievable.

Specify an equivalent you'd accept. Grades like S420 aren't rolled at 1.0 mm. Naming an equivalent (for example HC420LA) at RFQ stage avoids a re-quote later.

What drives press tool cost

  1. Number of operations

    Each pierce, notch, form or insert adds a station or a separate tool.

  2. Part size and strip width

    Larger parts need bigger die sets and larger presses.

  3. Material strength and thickness

    High-strength steels need tougher tool steels and stronger dies.

  4. Tolerances

    Tight form tolerances may need extra restrike or calibration stations.

  5. In-die operations

    Nut and stud insertion in the tool raises tooling cost but removes a handling step from every part.

  6. Tool life

    Annual volume × programme years sets the tool steel grade and inserts.

Ask for tooling to be quoted tool by tool, with checking fixtures and PPAP sampling stated. That's how every Genex quote is laid out.

  • 3D model (STEP or IGES) and 2D drawing with tolerances
  • Material specification and thickness, plus any accepted equivalents
  • Annual volume and programme life
  • Finish or coating specification
  • Secondary operations: fasteners, welding, assembly
  • PPAP level, packaging requirements and delivery location

Frequently asked questions

At what volume is progressive tooling worth it?

As a rule of thumb, from around 10,000 parts a year on a multi-year programme. Below roughly 2,000 a year, laser and folding is usually cheaper because there's no tooling. In between, stage or compound tooling is often the best balance.

What's the difference between progressive and compound tooling?

A progressive tool performs several operations at successive stations as a coil strip advances, producing a finished part every stroke. A compound tool performs several cutting operations, typically blanking and piercing, in one station and one stroke.

What drives the cost of a press tool?

Number of operations, part size and strip width, material strength and thickness, tolerances, in-die operations, and required tool life.

What PPAP level do I need?

Level 3 is the automotive default: warrant, samples and complete supporting data. Your customer's supplier manual will state the level they need.
