Materials

The right metal for the job.

Mild steel, coated steels, stainless steel, aluminium, copper and brass: what each is good for, which processes suit it, and what to watch for.

In short: Genex works mainly in mild steel, stainless steel and aluminium, plus coated steels such as pre-galvanised, and copper and brass for folded parts. Mild steel is the lowest-cost, most formable choice; stainless resists corrosion and suits food and medical use; aluminium saves weight. The right choice depends on where the part lives and what it has to do.

02 · Choosing

How to choose

  • Where does it live? Indoors, outdoors, wet, food contact or clean room changes the answer.
  • What does it carry? Strength and stiffness requirements decide grade and thickness.
  • Does weight matter? Aluminium saves weight but costs more per kilo.
  • Is it seen? Cosmetic parts may suit stainless or a powder coat.
  • What does your customer specify? Automotive and defence drawings often name the grade.

If you'd accept an equivalent grade, say so on the RFQ: it avoids a re-quote if your first choice isn't available in your thickness.

03 · Finishes

Finishes for each material

Mild steel usually needs protection: e-coat, zinc or zinc-nickel plating, or powder coat. Stainless is often left bare, polished or passivated. Aluminium can be left bare or powder coated. See metal finishes explained.
